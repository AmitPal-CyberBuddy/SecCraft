# Available Wireless Pentesting path — content audit

> **Historical baseline:** this audit describes the initial 27-lesson / 16-capture state. For the implemented follow-up (30 lessons / 18 captures), see `WIRELESS_CURRICULUM_IMPLEMENTATION.md`. The baseline judgments below are intentionally retained rather than rewritten as claims about unperformed field testing.

> **Historical snapshot:** This audit predates the module-ID consolidation. The current source has 15 wireless modules / 32 lessons and 22 self-review challenges; consult `frontend/src/content/` and the current pre-publication audit for present counts. The observations below refer to an earlier curriculum and must not be quoted as current release verification.

Scope: the shipped **20 modules / 27 lessons**, associated module quizzes, 15 challenges, 35 decision scenarios, 19 available labs and ENG-01. This is a source-and-artifact audit, not a claim that a beginner can perform an unsupervised professional engagement. No RF equipment, managed-client profiles, live AP, production topology, instructor grading or Northwind evidence bundle was available for this review. The 52-hour estimate is a catalogue estimate, not observed learning time. “Professional” names the later *methodology level*, not a verified competence credential. Challenges and most labs are self-review; only three labs have machine-checked answers; local quiz/XP completion does not verify field skill.

## Verdict on progression

The ordering is mostly sound: permission and RF concepts → 802.11 identity/frames → capture/tool boundaries → recon/evidence → legacy and modern Personal security → availability/look-alikes/guest → Enterprise EAP/RADIUS → integrated assessment and reporting. There is useful repetition of *evidence limits* across changing protocols, but too much early material assumes familiarity with RSNE, PMF, EAPOL, `tshark`, Linux and capture provenance before teaching their mechanics. Some later material is an operational reference rather than scaffolded teaching. The synthetic captures support reproducible protocol interpretation; they **do not** train monitor-mode troubleshooting, injection safety, client behavior, RF effects, real certificate validation, applied VLAN policy or performed retesting. The ENG-01 Northwind attachments do not exist, and the final fixture reuses earlier material. Accordingly this is a strong **capture-analysis and methodology foundation**, not a standalone beginner-to-independent-professional qualification.

For every entry below, “lesson” names the actual lesson, not a module title. Assessment refers to the existing lesson exercise, quiz, scenario, lab and/or challenge as applicable. These are content judgments, not scores assigned to learners.

## Assessment quality and alignment

- Every module now has a five-question knowledge check (20 banks, 100 questions). Newly authored 01/03/04 checks focus on evidence and choices; targeted replacements remove a dozen low-value tool/number recall items. Existing banks still mix basic recall with application; a correct multiple-choice answer is **not** proof of technique execution.
- The three answer-checked PCAP labs (02/05/06) genuinely validate some artifact extraction. The other 16 available labs and 15 challenges (45 self-review tasks) depend on honest learner self-review; scenarios provide model reasoning but cannot verify original evidence collection. Challenges are absent from 01/03/04/07/08/18/19, although those modules have lesson exercises or scenarios; 04 has no captured adapter capability and 07 has no WEP PCAP.
- Captures are generated and hash-checked, not real RF observations. Enterprise TLS, SAE, management-frame delivery, real portal enforcement and production segmentation are explicitly outside the artifact proof boundary. `methodology.pcapng` is not Northwind evidence.
- Technical corrections are important: an incorrect 802.11 state/EAP sequence, incorrect MS-CHAPv2 computation, false MAC attribution and an impossible handshake decryption task could have taught wrong operational decisions. The repaired lessons now distinguish advertisement, candidate verification, association state, receiver acceptance and verified impact.

## Four explicit outcomes

1. **Already good:** overall sequencing; authorization/safety discipline; packet-level references, manifests and reproducible synthetic captures; many meaningful evidence-limit scenarios; WPA/Enterprise and reporting scope; realistic distinction between observation and impact in later modules.
2. **Weak or missing at audit:** dense early glossaries and assumed prerequisites; three missing quizzes; recall-heavy questions; incorrect 802.11/EAP state model, MS-CHAPv2 formula, randomized-MAC inference and impossible decryption; an ENG-01 brief/metadata that implied absent Northwind evidence and retest; sparse independently graded tasks and no real RF/client/system outcomes.
3. **Improved here:** corrected those technical errors; added early concept-to-claim exercises and 15 application questions; replaced low-value recall questions; made the equipment/fixture boundary executable; aligned scenario and recon attribution; converted ENG-01 to honest scenario planning and an explicit multi-capture practicum; updated learning-path/module promises and curriculum-count verification.
4. **Still needed later:** prerequisite bridge for Linux/IP/subnets/tshark and worked byte decodes; safe owned-hardware RF labs with adapters, APs, clients and actual negotiated policies; coherent Enterprise EAP/RADIUS/managed-client and portal/segmentation artifacts; separate unseen capstone bundle including baseline, logs, configs and remediated retest; human/instructor review and trusted assessment before claiming professional field competence. These cannot be honestly simulated by adding more text or awarding XP.
