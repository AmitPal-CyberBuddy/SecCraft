# IEEE 802.11 Standard — PHY & MAC Deep Dive

## Learning Objectives
- Understand IEEE 802.11-2020 standard structure, amendments, PHY/MAC layers
- Learn Wi-Fi generations: 802.11a/b/g/n/ac/ax/be, data rates, modulation, MIMO, OFDMA, MLO
- Understand frequency bands, channels, regulatory domains, power limits
- Map amendments to beacon IEs: HT/VHT/HE/EHT capabilities
- Understand why PT cares about PHY/MAC — capabilities, misconfigurations, evidence

## Theory

### IEEE 802.11 Family

IEEE 802.11 is working group for Wireless LAN. Base standard 802.11-1997, then amendments, rolled up into 802.11-2020.

**Structure:**
- **PHY (Physical Layer):** RF, modulation, data rates, channels, bands
- **MAC (Medium Access Control):** Frame format, CSMA/CA, management/control/data frames, security, QoS

**Amendments:**
- **802.11a (1999):** 5 GHz, OFDM, 6-54 Mbps, 20 MHz
- **802.11b (1999):** 2.4 GHz, DSSS/CCK, 1/2/5.5/11 Mbps, 20 MHz
- **802.11g (2003):** 2.4 GHz, OFDM, 6-54 Mbps, backward compatible with b
- **802.11n (2009, Wi-Fi 4):** 2.4/5 GHz, MIMO-OFDM, up to 4 spatial streams, 40 MHz, 600 Mbps, HT (High Throughput)
- **802.11ac (2013, Wi-Fi 5):** 5 GHz only, 256-QAM, up to 8 streams, 80/160 MHz, MU-MIMO, 6.9 Gbps, VHT (Very High Throughput)
- **802.11ax (2021, Wi-Fi 6/6E):** 2.4/5/6 GHz, OFDMA, 1024-QAM, BSS coloring, 8 streams, 9.6 Gbps, HE (High Efficiency), 6 GHz U-NII-5-8
- **802.11be (2024, Wi-Fi 7):** 2.4/5/6 GHz, 4096-QAM, MLO (Multi-Link Operation), 320 MHz, 16 streams, 46 Gbps, EHT (Extremely High Throughput)

**Wi-Fi Alliance Generations:**
- Wi-Fi 4 = 802.11n, Wi-Fi 5 = 802.11ac, Wi-Fi 6 = 802.11ax 2.4/5 GHz, Wi-Fi 6E = 802.11ax 6 GHz, Wi-Fi 7 = 802.11be

**For PT:** Data rates irrelevant, but PHY affects beacon IEs — HT Capabilities (802.11n), VHT Capabilities (802.11ac), HE Capabilities (802.11ax), EHT Capabilities (802.11be). Misconfig like 40 MHz in 2.4 GHz is Medium finding.

### PHY Deep Dive

**Modulation:**
- **DSSS (802.11b):** Direct Sequence Spread Spectrum, 1/2/5.5/11 Mbps, robust but low rate
- **OFDM (802.11a/g):** Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing, 6/9/12/18/24/36/48/54 Mbps, divides channel into subcarriers, more robust
- **MIMO-OFDM (802.11n):** Multiple Input Multiple Output, multiple spatial streams (1-4), 40 MHz channel bonding, up to 600 Mbps, HT
- **256-QAM + MU-MIMO (802.11ac):** 256 Quadrature Amplitude Modulation (8 bits per symbol), Multi-User MIMO (AP to multiple clients simultaneously), 80/160 MHz, 8 streams, 6.9 Gbps, VHT
- **OFDMA + 1024-QAM (802.11ax):** Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiple Access, divides channel into RUs (Resource Units) for multiple clients, 1024-QAM (10 bits per symbol), BSS coloring (6-bit color to reduce interference), 9.6 Gbps, HE
- **MLO + 4096-QAM (802.11be):** Multi-Link Operation (client can use 2.4+5+6 GHz simultaneously), 4096-QAM (12 bits per symbol), 320 MHz, 16 streams, 46 Gbps, EHT

**Channel Width:**
- 20 MHz baseline, 1× 20 MHz
- 40 MHz = 2× 20 MHz bonded, doubles rate, but 2.4 GHz 40 MHz overlaps (only 1,6,11 non-overlapping with 20 MHz, 40 MHz uses 2 channels, causes interference) — Medium finding if enabled in 2.4 GHz
- 80 MHz = 4× 20 MHz, 5/6 GHz only, good
- 160 MHz = 8× 20 MHz, 5/6 GHz, very high rate, but DFS and interference
- 320 MHz = 16× 20 MHz, 6 GHz only, Wi-Fi 7, highest rate

**MCS (Modulation and Coding Scheme):**
- Index for modulation, coding rate, spatial streams, data rate
- Example: MCS 0 = BPSK 1/2, 6.5 Mbps 20 MHz 1 stream, MCS 7 = 64-QAM 5/6, 65 Mbps, MCS 9 = 256-QAM 5/6, 86.7 Mbps
- For PT: MCS irrelevant, but shown in beacon HT/VHT/HE capabilities

**Spatial Streams:**
- 1 stream = SISO, 2 streams = 2×2 MIMO, 3 streams = 3×3, 4 streams = 4×4, up to 8 streams 802.11ac, 16 streams 802.11be
- More streams = higher rate, but need multiple antennas
- For PT: Spatial streams shown in beacon, but not security relevant

### Frequency Bands & Regulatory

**2.4 GHz (2.400-2.4835 GHz):**
- Channels 1-14 (14 Japan only), 20 MHz each, center frequencies 2412 MHz Ch1, 2417 Ch2, ..., 2472 Ch12, 2484 Ch14
- Overlapping: Ch1 overlaps Ch2-5, Ch6 overlaps Ch2-10, Ch11 overlaps Ch7-13 — only 1,6,11 non-overlapping in US with 20 MHz
- 40 MHz in 2.4 GHz: Uses 2 channels, e.g., Ch6 HT40+ uses Ch6+Ch10, overlaps Ch1 and Ch11, causes interference — Medium finding
- Propagation: Good through walls, longer range (~50m indoor, 100m outdoor with 2 dBi), more interference (microwave 2.4 GHz, Bluetooth, Zigbee, all 2.4 GHz)
- Power: Typically 20 dBm (100 mW) EIRP max, varies by regdom

**5 GHz (5.150-5.825 GHz):**
- UNII bands: UNII-1 36-48 (5170-5250 MHz, 200 mW), UNII-2 52-64 (5250-5330 MHz, DFS, 200 mW), UNII-2e 100-144 (5490-5730 MHz, DFS, 200 mW), UNII-3 149-165 (5735-5835 MHz, 1W)
- 20/40/80/160 MHz channels, less overlapping, more channels (25× 20 MHz, 12× 40 MHz, 6× 80 MHz, 2× 160 MHz in US)
- DFS: Dynamic Frequency Selection — radar detection in UNII-2/2e, AP must vacate channel if radar detected, switch to another channel — recon should monitor multiple channels, AP may change channel
- Propagation: Less through walls than 2.4 GHz, shorter range (~25m indoor, 50m outdoor), less interference, higher rates
- Power: Up to 30 dBm (1W) in UNII-3, varies by band and regdom

**6 GHz (5.925-7.125 GHz, Wi-Fi 6E/7):**
- U-NII-5 1-93 (5925-6425 MHz, 59× 20 MHz), U-NII-6 95-111 (6425-6525 MHz, 8× 20 MHz), U-NII-7 113-173 (6525-6875 MHz, 30× 20 MHz), U-NII-8 175-233 (6875-7125 MHz, 29× 20 MHz)
- 59× 20 MHz, 29× 40 MHz, 14× 80 MHz, 7× 160 MHz, 3× 320 MHz (Wi-Fi 7)
- Only WPA3 allowed — no WPA2, no WEP, no TKIP, open must be OWE or WPA3 — security benefit, no transition downgrade if pure 6 GHz
- Propagation: Even less through walls than 5 GHz, shortest range (~15m indoor, 30m outdoor), cleanest spectrum, highest rates, less interference
- Power: Up to 36 dBm EIRP with AFC (Automated Frequency Coordination) for standard power, 30 dBm low power indoor

**Regulatory Domain (regdom):**
- Country code determines allowed channels, power, DFS, 6 GHz
- `iw reg get` shows regdom, `iw reg set US` sets US
- Example: US allows Ch1-11 2.4 GHz, Ch36-165 5 GHz, Ch1-233 6 GHz with AFC, EU allows Ch1-13 2.4 GHz, Ch36-140 5 GHz, Ch1-233 6 GHz low power
- For PT: Check regdom, may affect channel scanning — if regdom JP, Ch14 allowed, if US, Ch12-13 not allowed with 20 dBm

### MAC Layer — Frame Format

**802.11 Frame:**
```
Octets: 2 Frame Control, 2 Duration/ID, 6 Address1, 6 Address2, 6 Address3, 2 Seq Control, 6 Address4 (optional), 0-2304 Frame Body, 4 FCS

Frame Control (2 bytes, 16 bits):
- Protocol Version (2 bits): 0
- Type (2 bits): 0 Management, 1 Control, 2 Data
- Subtype (4 bits): Beacon 8, Probe Req 4, Probe Resp 5, Auth 11, Assoc Req 0, Assoc Resp 1, Deauth 12, Disassoc 10, QoS Data 8, EAPOL (Data subtype with LLC)
- To DS (1 bit): To Distribution System
- From DS (1 bit): From DS
- More Frag (1 bit): More fragments
- Retry (1 bit): Retransmission
- Power Mgmt (1 bit): Power save
- More Data (1 bit): More data buffered
- Protected Frame (1 bit): Encrypted (WEP/WPA/WPA2/WPA3)
- +HTC/Order (1 bit): HT Control / Order

Addresses:
- Address1: Receiver (RA) — usually DA or BSSID
- Address2: Transmitter (TA) — usually SA or BSSID
- Address3: BSSID or SA/DA depending on To/From DS
- Address4: Optional, for WDS (To DS=1 From DS=1)

Frame Body: Management IE, Control, Data (LLC + IP + ...)
FCS: Frame Check Sequence, CRC32
```

**Types:**
- **Management (Type 0):** Beacon (8), Probe Req (4), Probe Resp (5), Auth (11), Assoc Req (0), Assoc Resp (1), Deauth (12), Disassoc (10), Action (13)
- **Control (Type 1):** RTS (11), CTS (12), ACK (13), Block Ack (9)
- **Data (Type 2):** Data (0), QoS Data (8), Null (4), QoS Null (12), EAPOL (Data with LLC 0x888E)

**For PT:** Frame Control type/subtype determines Wireshark filter — `wlan.fc.type==0` management, `wlan.fc.type_subtype==8` beacon, `wlan.fc.type_subtype==12` deauth, `eapol` for 4-way handshake.

## VAPT Relevance

- **Recon:** 2.4/5/6 GHz bands have different channels, need to scan all — `iw dev wlan0 scan` or Kismet or airodump-ng with channel hopping

## Evidence Collection

- Beacon: Channel, band, width, PHY capabilities (HT 20/40, VHT 80/160, HE 20/40/80/160), MCS, spatial streams, vendor, signal
- Example: `Beacon SSID LAB-WIFI BSSID AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF Ch6 2.4 GHz 20 MHz HT 20 MHz only MCS 0-7 1 stream WPA2-PSK CCMP`

## Attack → Defense → Retest

- **Attack:** Observe 2.4 GHz AP with HT 40 MHz enabled, causes overlap, interference, bad practice
- **Defense:** Use 20 MHz only in 2.4 GHz — `ht_capab=[HT20]`, use 5/6 GHz for 40/80/160 MHz, check regdom
- **Retest:** New beacon, verify HT capabilities show 20 MHz only, no 40 MHz, `wlan_mgt.ht.capabilities` shows 20 MHz

## Interactive Check

> Beacon shows `HT Capabilities: HT20/HT40, SM Power Save disabled, RX Greenfield, RX HT20 SGI, RX HT40 SGI, TX STBC, RX STBC 1-stream, Max AMSDU length 3839, DSSS/CCK HT40, PSMP, 40 MHz Intolerant, L-SIG TXOP protection`. What does it mean and is it misconfig?

Answer: HT Capabilities show HT20 and HT40 supported, SGI (Short Guard Interval) supported, STBC (Space-Time Block Coding), AMSDU, DSSS/CCK HT40, etc. If this is 2.4 GHz AP with HT40 enabled, it's Medium finding bad practice — should be HT20 only in 2.4 GHz. Check channel — if Ch6 HT40+, it uses Ch6+Ch10, overlaps Ch1 and Ch11. Defense: `ht_capab=[HT20]` for 2.4 GHz. Evidence: Beacon frame, HT capabilities IE, channel.
